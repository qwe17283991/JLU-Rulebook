# Plot Points, Editorial Points & the Crisis Die

## PLOT POINTS

Whenever things are dire, a Hero can call for the oldest forces known in the universe: the Plot. Plot Points are the narrative tool that grant players the power to twist fate itself. They can be used to enact a wide range of effects — usually the ones that would otherwise feel a little too convenient.

#### Using Plot Points

Every player starts the game with 0 Plot Points, but they can be acquired through the usage of abilities and at the Editor’s discretion, typically whenever a Hero pulls of an outrageous comic-bookish stunt. But be cautious, using Plot Ploints may stress the very foundation of the universe, triggering the **Crisis Die**.

When you spend a Plot Point, choose one of the following effects (but do not feel limited to them):

**Guarantee Success**: Turn a failure into a success. Did you roll the dice and come up short? Spend a Plot Point. Not only do you succeed, you do so in spectacular fashion. Alternatively, you may spend it before rolling to add +5 to the result.

**Plot Armor**: You face a blow that should have been the end. Spend a Plot Point to completely negate an attack or effect that would have targeted you, describing how you narrowly escaped.

**Rewrite the Script (or Retcon)**: Change a fact already established in the story. Spend a Plot Point to introduce one of the following twists:

- **“I prepared for this”**: You reveal that you have been carrying a specific item (not a superpowered one) all along.

- **“Luckily, I know someone”**: An old ally or contact unexpectedly appears to offer help.

- **“Wait, I know their weakness!”**: You remember a crucial detail, revealing a new temporary Limitation on the villain.

**Power Increase**: You push beyond your Limits. Spend **1 to 3 Plot Points**. For the rest of the scene, for each point spent, treat one of your Powers as though it were **one Grade higher**. At the end of the scene, mark a number of Conditions equal to the number of points you spent.

**Introduce a Fact**: Add a convenient detail to the scene. Spend a Plot Point and declare a plausible fact about the environment that gives you an advantage during that scene.

## EDITORIAL POINTS

The Editor has their own way of shaping the narrative: the **Editorial Points**. Whenever a player uses a Plot Point, an Editorial Point enters the Editor’s arsenal. The Editor may hold a maximum number of Editorial Points equal to the number of players and may spend only **one per Scene**, ensuring that their use remains impactful without becoming oppressive.

#### Introducing an Editorial Complication

When the Editor spends an **Editorial Point**, they may introduce an **Editorial Complication** by choosing one of the following effects:

###### Editor’s Note:

The Editor adds an immediate twist to the scene. Think of those moments in comic books when everything seems fine, the villain has been defeated, and the day has been saved.

Then something happens! Henchmen, a clone, an even more powerful villain: there are countless possibilities.

###### “This is my True Form!”

The form the villain has held up till now was not their final one. In truth, they were holding back all along. This is their real and more powerful form.

*The Toyman who has just been defeated reveals himself to be (yes, you guessed it) a mechatronic toy built to deceive the Heroes. From afar, you hear laughter and the sound of heavy robotic footsteps: he is piloting a giant doll-shaped robot.*

###### “He has a new trick!”

The villain reveals they have a power or piece of equipment they had not previously made use of.

*Example*: The Heroes have easily defeated the Penguin’s henchmen. The Editor spends an Editorial Point and announces:

> The moment the last henchman falls, one of the penguin statues in the hall opens up, revealing an automatic machine-gun turret.

###### Villainous Monologue:

The villain pauses the action to make a speech. They reveal part of their plan or mock the Heroes. While they speak, the Heroes are momentarily paralyzed by the drama. At the end of the monologue, the villain recovers all of their **Resolve** points.

*Example*: Lex Luthor’s armor is damaged. The Editor spends an Editorial Point:

Luthor laughs over the comms.

> You think you have won? Fools.
>
> While you were busy with my toy, my real plan was already unfolding…

The Editor then restores all of Luthor’s **Resolve** points.

###### Introduce a Surprise Element:

A mysterious figure enters the scene, not necessarily as an enemy. They may be a rival of the villain, a civilian with a secret or a third faction.

*Example*: Wonder Woman is about to defeat Cheetah. The Editor spends an Editorial Point:

> Suddenly, from the shadows, a group of assassins from the League of Shadows emerges.
>
> One of them turns to Cheetah and says, ‘Ra’s al Ghul requires the artifact, cursed one.’ What was a two-sided fight has now become a three-way standoff.

## CRISIS DIE

The history of the DC Universe is permeated by crises: catastrophic events that completely change the status quo. The Crisis Die is a d20 containing 20 random and often catastrophic twists that can upend the story like time travelling, villains returning, and even power shifting!

#### Rolling the Crisis Die:

When the Heroes overuse the power of the narrative, the universe reacts. At that point, the Crisis Die is triggered. Whenever a player uses a Plot Point, they must roll a d6: If the result is equal or inferior to the number of Plot Points used on that Issue, a Crisis Die is triggered. The Editor rolls 1D20, compares the result to the table on page 7, and applies the relevant effect. After that, Plot Points cannot be used again during that Issue.

**Example**: *During the final battle with Bane, the Hero Ice Fist receives a shattering blow. Refusing to fall, Ice Fist declares that his old master taught him the Spine-Enhancer Technique! The Editor grants Ice Fist a Plot Point for his impressive creativity, and the Hero uses it as Plot Armor!*

*During this Issue, the Heroes have used Plot Points a total of three times, with Ice Fist’s use being the third. A d6 is rolled, and the result is **2, which is lower than the number of Plot Points** used in this Issue, triggering the Crisis Die.*

*The Editor rolls the Crisis Die and gets a 5: **Catastrophe!** The ground begins to crack and split apart as a massive earthquake hits Gotham City!*

#### Crisis Die Table

|  | Crisis Die Result | Effect |
| :---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Power Loss!** | Until the end of the **Issue**, the group of Heroes lose all of their powers. Powers that do not depend on external sources or genetic factors, such as martial arts, remain. |
| 2 | **Identity Crisis!** | Some strange affliction has befallen the Heroes. Until the end of the **Scene**, they must swap character sheets with the player to their left. |
| 3 | **I, Evil!** | A dark version of one of the Heroes appears! |
| 4 | **Multiple Crises!** | Roll **2D20** on this table. Whatever the results, they happen simultaneously. |
| 5 | **Catastrophe!** | A natural disaster such as a tsunami, earthquake, or hurricane threatens the city! |
| 6 | **Negative Public Opinion!** | A fresh newspaper story, perhaps not too concerned with the truth, has just torn the team apart. |
| 7 | **Meta Crisis!** | Somehow, the inhabitants of the area have gained metahuman powers! That is definitely going to be a problem. |
| 8 | **Villain Ally!** | An enemy temporarily joins the group. What are their true intentions? |
| 9 | **An Old Enemy Returns!** | Somehow… They returned. |
| 10 | **Time Traveler!** | A time traveler appears. They bring bombastic news! |
| 11 | **Portal to the Past!** | A portal to the distant past opens! It may unleash dinosaurs, Roman gladiators, Vikings, or even pull the Heroes into the Old West! |
| 12 | **Heroic Betrayal!** | An ally of the Heroes turns against them. What could be the reason for this treachery? |
| 13 | **Alien Invasion!** | A ship arrives on Earth and makes the announcement: an invasion is coming, and all Earthlings must surrender. Dominators? Parademons? |
| 14 | **It Was an Illusion!** | The most recent events of the last Scene were an illusion. Perhaps caused by magic, holograms, or even memory manipulation. |
| 15 | **It Was a Clone!** | An important **Editor Character** turns out to be a clone! |
| 16 | **Involuntary Teleportation!** | A boom tube opens, taking the group of Heroes to some unknown location. |
| 17 | **Portal to the Future!** | A portal to the future opens! Out come futuristic robots, an alternate-timeline version of one of the Heroes, or the Legion of Super-Heroes in need of help! |
| 18 | **Judgment!** | An organized group wants to put the Heroes on trial for crises they supposedly caused! |
| 19 | **Gods on Earth!** | Entities such as the New Gods, the Greek pantheon, or some lesser deity appear! |
| 20 | **New Powers!** | The Heroes develop new abilities: until the end of the **Issue**, they gain a new power, chosen by the Editor. |
