# Status, Movement & Combat Actions

## STATUS

Some abilities may cause a wide variety of effects in a Hero. Unlike Conditions, Heroes do not have a Limit on the number of Statuses they can receive, but two identical Statuses do not stack.

#### Shaken

Makes any **Mind** or **Spirit** test with **Disadvantage**.

#### Asleep

Cannot perform actions. If they suffer any type of Damage, they wake immediately.

#### Frightened

Suffers **Disadvantage** on all tests against the source of fear, and cannot willingly move closer to it.

#### Stunned

Loses **1 Action** on their turn.

#### Prone

The character must spend **1 Movement Actio**n to stand or regain their footing before they can use any more Movement Actions.

While **Prone**:

- They cannot move.

- They make all attacks (melee and ranged) with **Disadvantage**.

#### Blinded

Suffers **Disadvantage** on all actions that depend on sight.

#### Frozen

Is **Immobilized** and suffers **1 Damage per Round**.

#### Confused

At the start of the turn, roll **1d6**:

- **1 to 4:** Attacks the nearest ally with all available actions.

- **5 to 6:** Acts normally, but all actions cost **double Resolve**.

#### Controlled

Is under the control of another character. During their turn, whoever controls them chooses their actions.

#### Weakened

Disadvantage on **Potency Tests**.

#### Poisoned

Is **Engulfed** in poison. Suffers the effects of **Engulfed** (see below).

#### Engulfed

Suffers **2 Damage per Round** due to poison, flames, bleeding, or another ongoing effect.

#### Exhausted

Cannot recover **Resolve** through rest or abilities.

Suffers **Disadvantage** on all **Potency, Resistance**, and **Mind** tests.

#### Immobilized

Cannot move. May act normally, as long as movement is not required. May attempt to break free.

#### Slowed

ALL **Movement Actions** cost **+1 additional Action**.

#### Burned

Is **Engulfed** in flames. Suffers the effects of **Engulfed**.

#### Bleeding

Loses **2 Resolve per Tier** at the start of each turn.

#### Vulnerable

Has no **Resolve**. Any amount of Damage causes them to mark a **Condition Box**.

## MOVEMENT

Knowing how to position yourself can be the difference between the success and failure of a mission. In a setting with runners capable of surpassing the speed of light, artifacts that teleport in the blink of an eye, acrobatic fighters and warriors who fly with metal wings, movement is essential.

### Zone

A **Zone** is the area where the action is taking place. An abandoned factory, a laboratory complex, the skies of **Metropolis**, or anything similar: it represents the place where everything is happening. It is not uncommon for there to be several Zones in a Scene, if the characters are fighting through the skies of the **City of Tomorrow**, they are in one Zone, while the **Daily Planet** building below them may be considered another distinct one.

### Distances

Even in an abstract way, it is still important to define distances within the same Zone. The distances are **Adjacent, Close, Far**, and **Distant**.

**Adjacent:** Within arm’s reach, generally engaged in melee combat. It is not necessary to spend movement actions to interact with a target that is Adjacent.

**Close:** Nearby, a few steps or leaps away. It is necessary to spend one movement action to bring a Close target to an Adjacent distance.

**Far:** Still visible, but requires a greater effort to get there. It is necessary to spend two movement actions to bring a Far target to an Adjacent distance.

**Distant:** Practically at the other end of a Zone. It is necessary to spend **three movement actions** to bring a **Distant** target to an **Adjacent** distance.

| **Target Distance** | **Actions Required** |
| --- | --- |
| Adjacent | 0 |
| Close | 1 |
| Far | 2 |
| Distant | 3 |

Powers such as **Super Speed** or **Speed Force** usually reduce the need to spend movement actions to reach an **Adjacent** distance from the target.

Ranged attacks, such as **Energy Beams**, thrown objects, and firearms, reach targets at different distances. Higher-Grade Powers can reach farther, as can more modern weapons and advanced technologies.

## COMBAT ACTIONS

During combat, each character has **3 Actions** in a **Round**. These include Attacks, movement, the use of powers and strategic assistance.

Actions represent everything a character can do in that span of time, and may be divided however the player wishes: move to an enemy, attack them, then pull away; attack twice in a row and activate a power or ability; use a gadget, attack, then move. Thanks to this flexibility, each Turn can be used in a tactical and creative way, promoting dynamic scenarios worthy of a comic book story.

### The Three-Action Economy

As explained earlier, during a **Round**, a character has access to **3 Actions**. They may be used all at once during the Turn, or they may be reserved for **Reactions**, which are a specific type of Action that breaks Initiative order.

As a rule, Actions are not interchangeable between different Rounds; a character cannot, for example, “bring forward” their Actions from the next Round into the current one, gaining six Actions and then having none in the following Round. In addition, Actions not used by the end of that Round are lost.

### Reaction

This is a specific type of Action performed in response to another, requiring a trigger, such as actively blocking a strike or getting out of the way of a shot. To use a **Reaction**, the character must have at least **one Action** remaining from their total that Round.

### Attack

Cost: 1 Action

An Attack action involves a direct blow, such as a punch, a shot, or a sword slash. When a Hero makes an Attack, they make an **Attack Test** against the target’s **Defense value**. If successful, they deal Damage directly to the target’s **Resolve**.

###### Attack Test

When making an **Attack Test**, always roll **1d20** **+ Accuracy + Attack Bonus** to determine the result. In most cases, an Attack Test will always be compared to the target’s **Defense**.

###### Melee Attack

This is made using hands or melee weapons. It generally occurs when two opponents are at **Adjacent** distance.

###### Ranged Attack

This type of attack involves projectiles such as bullets, arrows, blasts of fire, or laser beams. It is made against targets that are **one or more distances away** from a Hero. Depending on the Grade of the Power, the quality of the weapon, or the technology involved, an Attack may reach **Close, Far,** or even **Distant** targets.

Unless explicitly stated otherwise, **Ranged Attacks** suffer **Disadvantage** when made at **Adjacent** distance.

###### Splash Page

In an Attack, a **Splash Page** only occurs if the character exceeds the target’s **Defense** by **10 or more**. When that happens, the number of dice rolled for **Damage** is doubled.

###### Multiple Attacks

A character may use as many available Actions as they have to attack in a turn, but if they do, they risk lowering their combat accuracy. Each additional Attack beyond the first causes the character to suffer –5 (cumulative with each subsequent attack) on their **Attack Tests** until the beginning of their next turn. A character’s Attack count resets on their next turn, which means Attacks made as **Reactions** outside their Turn still count toward the total.

Penalty modifiers from different sources do not stack. If a character has one ability that changes the penalty to **–2 instead of –5** and another that changes it to **–3 instead of –5**, the character uses whichever is more favorable.

| **Attack** | **Penalty** |
| --- | --- |

| 2nd Attack | –5 |
| --- | --- |
| 3rd Attack | –10 |
| 4th Attack | –15 |
| 5th Attack | –20 |

### Activation

Cost: Variable

Activating a **Power, Maneuver, Archetype Ability,** and similar effects requires an **Activation**. As a rule, this requires **1 Action**, but that number may vary as indicated in the description.

### Dodge

Cost: 1 Action (Reaction)

A **Dodge** occurs when a Hero quickly moves out of the way of an incoming attack. When that happens, the Hero’s **Agility** is added to their **Defense** until the end of the **Turn**.

### Block

Cost: 1 Action (Reaction)

A **Block** occurs when a Hero prepares to absorb the impact of an attack, increasing their Defenses. When a Hero uses **Block**, they add **Resistance** to their **Defense** against the next incoming **Attack**.

### Extend Range

Cost: 2 Actions

A Hero may take a deep breath and prepare to make a **Ranged Attack** with a weapon; the Hero may extend the range by **1 Distance**, spending **2 Resolve**.

### Disarm

Cost: 3 Actions

You may remove a weapon that the target is carrying. Make an **Opposed Test** against the target. If the action involves brutally tearing a sword from the target’s hands, use **Potency**; if you shoot the target directly in the hand so that they drop the pistol, use **Accuracy**. There are countless ways to disarm someone, and virtually any Attribute may be used in this situation. The target decides which Attribute they will use in the Opposed Test.

### Help

**Cost: 1 Action + Movement Actions**

Often on the battlefield, Heroes need to assist their allies. The **Help** action includes rescuing, moving someone out of the way, or even applying medical care to fallen allies. **Help** is an umbrella term that includes all of these possibilities.

### Move

**Cost: 1 Action + Movement Actions**

Sometimes an ally is unable to leave a dangerous place, such as the edge of a volcano about to erupt, and that is when you fly, run, or even pilot a vehicle toward them. You spend Movement Actions normally to reach your ally, and **+1 Action** to relocate them up to **1 Distance** from your point of origin.

### Cover

Cost: 1 Action

You provide strategic cover for your ally, whether through a hail of bullets, a smoke bomb, or a powerful gust of wind. The next Attack against your ally suffers **Disadvantage**.

### Defend

Cost: 3 Actions

You strategically position yourself to defend an ally **Adjacent** to you, placing yourself between them and their attacker. Until the beginning of your next turn, Attacks made against that ally suffer Disadvantage, while Attacks made against you gain **Advantage**.

## THE DOOMSDAY CLOCK

In **Rescue scenes, missions**, or even long-term villainous plans, the Editor draws a circle and divides it into several parts: this is the **Doomsday Clock**. It is a tool that represents the progress and complexity of danger in a Rescue scene.

Each time the Heroes fail, the group takes too long to act, or an external force such as a villain or a catastrophe makes things worse, the Editor advances the clock as a way of showing that the danger is approaching and drastic consequences are near.

### Completing the Clock

When completely filled, the clock triggers the worst possible scenario, and the Heroes fail. The civilians are not saved, the bomb explodes, and the catastrophe is not contained. Now the Heroes must deal with the consequences, such as public opinion, the weight of failure, and the villains completing their objectives.

#### Four-Part Clock:

*The Hospital Explosion*

A four-part Clock represents moderate challenges, but ones that require coordination and teamwork to resolve.

A bomb has been planted in a downtown Hospital. They must act quickly before the worst happens!

###### First Part Filled

Panic sets in when the alarms sound, and the population rushes out in desperation.

###### Second Part Filled

Smoke and soot spread throughout the place, making action more difficult.

###### Third Part Filled

Only a few minutes remain before the bomb explodes. The Heroes need to find it as fast as possible!

Henchmen appear to cause even more confusion.

###### Fourth Part Filled

The Heroes fail to stop the explosion. Many civilians are injured and the hospital’s structure is damaged.

Each time a Hero fails a **Heroic Action** in **Rescue scenes** or during a mission, one part of the Clock is filled. When the Clock fills all its parts, the Heroes fail the Rescue, and the worst happens.

## HEROIC ACTIONS

Like combat, Rescue Scenes are divided into Turns and Rounds. Heroic Actions are broad and varied, encompassing a different number of maneuvers and stunts a Hero can perform. On their Turn, a Hero can perform up to three Heroic Actions, and can choose how many of it they will use: the bigger the number of actions spent on a Heroic Action, the bigger are the chances of succeeding.

###### Number of Actions

A Hero can choose to focus on several different tasks, such as rushing into a burning building to rescue falling civilians, putting out the flames, and still coordinating firefighters in a joint effort to contain the rest of the fire. This is entirely possible, but the more different tasks a Hero attempts to perform in this way, the more difficult their job becomes.

In JLU RPG, the more a Hero focuses on a single objective during a Rescue Scene, the better, after all, teams work together to cover as much ground as possible. For each Action a Hero spends focusing on the same Heroic Action, their chances of success increase.

**1 Action:** If a Hero uses one of their Actions to save the civilians from the burning building, they make the roll against the Tension established by the Editor normally.

**2 Actions:** If the Hero uses two of their three available Actions to save the civilians from the burning building, they gain Advantage on the Test.

**3 Actions:** If the Hero uses all three of their Actions to save the civilians from the burning building, no Test is required, and the Hero treats their result as a 20 for the purposes of the Test. However, if a result of 20 is still insufficient to meet the required Tension number, the action is considered a failure.

### Heroic Effort

In a **Rescue Scene**, a Hero may go beyond their Limits, putting themselves at risk to save those in need. **Heroic Effort** allows the Hero to mark a **Condition** in order to receive an **Extra Action** on their Turn or recover an amount of **Resolve** equal to their **Tier base**.

#### Examples of Heroic Actions

###### Remove Debris

Buildings collapse, bridges fall. Situations like these require a Potency or **Resistance Test** against **Tension**. **Failure:** Lose **2 Resolve**.

###### Calm the Population

Panic is one of the great dangers in risky situations, and preventing the crowd from losing control is essential.

**Test:** Spirit vs. Tension

**Failure:** Mark the **Stunned** Status.

###### Command the Crowd

Bringing a crowd to safety in an organized way is essential to ensure the success of a rescue and avoid harm.

**Test:** Mind vs. Tension

**Failure:** Mark one Condition.

###### Save the Falling

The critical moment when civilians are falling from collapsing places or hanging from a precipice requires quick action.

**Test:** Agility vs. Tension

**Failure:** Lose **5 Resolve**; if you have no Resolve, mark **two Conditions**.

###### Contain Environmental Threat

A fire spreads, a chemical leak fills the air with toxic gases, or high-voltage cables come loose, threatening to electrocute everyone. The Hero must use their powers or quick thinking to isolate or neutralize the danger.

**Test:** Mind or Spirit vs. Tension **Failure:** The Hero is caught by the danger. Gain the “**Engulfed**” Status (burned, poisoned, etc.), and the **Doomsday Clock** advances **one extra segment**.

###### Disarm Device

A time bomb is about to explode, a nuclear reactor is overloading, or an unstable portal threatens to suck in everything around it. Time is running out, and a steady hand or a brilliant mind is required.

**Test:** Mind or Accuracy vs. Tension **Failure:** The device becomes even more unstable. The **Doomsday Clock** advances **two segments immediately**.

## RECOVERY

Heroic life takes its toll, and even the mightiest of Heroes still suffers harm after an intense mission or brutal combat. Even if you are made for action, recovery is still necessary.

###### Recovering Resolve

Resolve is the resource Heroes will use most. Recovering it is simple: all spent Resolve is restored at the beginning of a new **Scene**, as long as the Hero pauses to clear their head, take a deep breath, and prepare for another challenge. In other words, Heroes need to go through an **Interlude Moment** to recover their Resolve points.

If an **Interlude Moment** turns into a **Danger** or **Combat** scene, at the Editor’s discretion, Heroes recover only **half** of their Resolve.

###### Recovering Conditions

Conditions do not recover as easily as Resolve. Of course, **Scratched** and **Tired** disappear at the end of a Scene, but the rest of the Conditions require the Heroes to recover in a more elaborate way. At least one **Interlude Moment** focused on healing is necessary, in which the Heroes withdraw to a safe place and recover their strength. Here, if the **Interlude Moment** is interrupted by some danger or villain, the Conditions remain.

## DEATH

It is already a well-worn comic-book cliché: no one really dies, and when it happens, they do not stay dead for long. Deaths in **JLU RPG** are rare, and even if a Hero is **Defeated** by a powerful enemy, they still live to see another day.

A Hero only dies if the player decides that this is the appropriate moment for their departure. Maybe it was while fighting a powerful enemy, sacrificing themselves to defeat evil, or they spent all their energy saving innocents from disaster and perished in the process. Sacrifice and altruism are intrinsic to Heroes, and the death of a character can provide continuity for many future **Issues**.
