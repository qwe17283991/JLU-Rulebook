    Tests & Combat Basics¶

    ROLLING THE DICE¶

    The core mechanic governing moments of uncertainty in JLU RPG is the roll of a twenty-sided die (d20). Whenever a Hero tries to perform an action whose result is uncertain, they must make a Test. The formula is simple:

    Test Result = 1d20 + Relevant Attribute + Other Bonuses (Powers, abilities, equipment, etc.)

    This result is then compared to a target number defined by the Editor, known as Tension. One important note for players: the player never asks for Tests; they always suggest a course of action, and the Editor decides the appropriate Test and Attributes.

    Advantage¶

    Certain abilities or favorable situations may grant Advantage to a character. When this happens, the player rolls two dice (d20 for Tests, or the Damage Die for attacks) and keeps the better result. If for some reason a Hero gains more than one Advantage, they do not increase the number of dice rolled, but instead gain a +2 bonus for each Advantage beyond the first. Advantage and Disadvantage cancel each other out.

    Disadvantage¶

    Similar to Advantage, there are some unfavorable situations that complicate a Hero’s life. When this happens, they suffer Disadvantage. The player rolls two dice and keeps the worse result.

    Increase by One Step¶

    Some abilities allow you to increase your Tier Die by one step. When you do this, follow the order below. Increasing the Die beyond d12 means rolling a d12 with Advantage.

    d6 → d8 → d12

    Actions¶

    Justice League Unlimited is a game built around Actions: throughout the game, characters will perform many of them. Flying quickly to save endangered innocents, landing the final shot on the villain who’s terrorising the city, calming a riotous crowd, and many other efforts are all examples of Actions. Think of them as a small cause that generates a narrative effect in a game situation.

    Actions are widely used in combat, rescue scenes, social interactions, and environmental challenges... the list is virtually endless. At all times during the game, Heroes (and villains) will be performing Actions.

    TENSION¶

    Tension is the Editor’s main tool for defining the difficulty of any situation in the game. It is a numerical value that represents the danger and complexity of a task. The final word on the value of Tension always belongs to the Editor, who may adjust it based on various narrative factors.

    DIFFICULTY LEVELS¶

    In Justice League Unlimited RPG, the division by Tiers allows the game to cover a wide variety of challenges appropriate to the many power levels a comic-book story encompasses. While a group of Year One Heroes deals with the chaos of the streets during an alien invasion, the World’s Finest deals directly with the intergalactic conqueror. For this reason, the Difficulty Levels (Routine, Challenging, Difficult, Heroic, and Impossible) have values calculated to better suit each Tier.

    Routine: A task that a Hero of the corresponding Tier should be able to accomplish with some ease, though failure is still possible.
    Tier D Example (Tension 12): Stop a gang of ordinary thieves from robbing an ATM.

    Challenging: The standard for heroic life. It represents a task that requires effort and presents a real risk of complications.
    Tier C Example (Tension 17): Stop falling debris from hitting civilians on a busy street.

    Difficult: A task that will likely require planning, teamwork, or intelligent use of powers. Failure here has serious consequences.
    Tier C Example (Tension 20): Put together the clues left by a serial killer like the Riddler.

    Tension Tier D Tier C
    Routine 12 15
    Challenging 15 18
    Difficult 18 21
    Heroic 21 25
    Impossible 26+ 28+

    TESTS¶

    A Test occurs whenever there is an uncertainty in the outcome of an Action, so the dice are rolled against the Tension set by the Editor to resolve the uncertainty.

    Success!: When a Hero obtains a value equal to or greater than the Tension, they achieve a Success.

    Critical Success: Splash Page!: You know that incredible comic-book scene drawn by the artist you love, in full double-spread style? Here it works the same way. When a Hero exceeds the Tension by 15 or more or rolls a natural 20 on the d20 (that is, the value 20 on the die, without any bonuses), a Splash Page occurs. This is an epic moment in which the best possible result happens.

    Failure: When a Hero gets a result lower than the Tension, we have a failure. The Editor describes how, just as the speedster was about to catch the Villain, they escape at the last instant and the portal closes.

    Critical Failure: Editorial Cut!: When a Hero fails by 15 or more below the Tension, or rolls a natural 1 on the d20, we have a Critical Failure. Not only does the Hero fail their Test, but the worst happens. The Editor narrates how this disaster unfolds: perhaps the attempt to hold the truck falling off the bridge did not go as expected and now the Amazon is being dragged down with it, in freefall! How will she get out of this?

    Close Call!: When the Hero fails to reach the Tension, but chooses to spend Resolve, they may spend Resolve points to increase the Test result. The result increases by 1 for each Resolve point spent. Close Call! cannot be used on Editorial Cuts.

    OPPOSED TEST¶

    When two forces enter into direct conflict, such as a race around the globe between two speedsters, an arm-wrestling match between the strongest beings on Earth, or a chess game between a genius and a quantum supercomputer, an Opposed Test is made. In an Opposed Test, both sides make a Test using the relevant attribute. Whoever gets the higher result wins the contest. In case of a tie, a new Test must be made. Note that an Opposed Test is not made against Tension, but rather against another result, and therefore effects that use or depend on Tension cannot be used.

    COMBAT¶

    Sometimes heroic work involves getting into a fight, after all, there are enemies who will not be convinced by words alone. Combat in JLU RPG is designed to be fast, dynamic and cinematic, but with a touch of tactics. Using the 3 Actions mechanic, Heroes will devise strategies, apply unusual plans and kick the backsides of many evildoers.

    Stages of Combat¶

    Initiative¶

    When a conflict begins, all parties involved compare their Agility score to decide who acts first. The order of turns is determined from the highest to the lowest. In case of a tie, both parts involved must make an Agility Test, and the winner is placed immediately above the loser.

    Increasing Initiative: A character can force their Limits to increase their place on the Initiative order: By each Resolve point spent, they receive +1 during the Initiative phase.

    Inspire¶

    Activation: 1

    Some Heroes have a natural aura of command, fulfilling those around them with hope and determination. With this action, a Hero can inspire allies, pushing them beyond their Limits. To use this Action, the Spirit of the Hero must be higher than the target’s Spirit score. A target can only be affected by one of Inspire’s effects at a time.

    Once per round, choose one ally that can see or hear you, and make a Spirit Test against the Tension set by the Editor. On a Success, choose on of the following:

    You’ve Got This!: The target receives Advantage on their next Heroic Action.

    Don’t Give Up!: A target receives a bonus to their next Attack, Block or Dodge equal to your Spirit value, as long as they are facing a threat with a Tier Higher than theirs.

    Heroes Never Quit!: You can spend 1 Resolve Point to grant a target 1 Resolve Point, up to a maximum number of Resolve Points per Scene equal to your Spirit score.

    Tactics¶

    Activation: 1

    With this action, a Hero can shout orders and commands to their allies and civilians,acting like a true oracle and granting them tactical advantages. To use this Action, the Mind Attribute of the Hero must be higher than the target’s Mind. A target can only be affected by one Tactics effect at a time.

    Once per Round, Choose one ally that can hear you and make a Mind Test against the Tension set by the Editor, on a Success, choose one of the following:

    On your left!!: The target’s next Dodge receives a bonus equal to your Mind.

    Engage!: Before the beginning of a Combat, the Target’s Initiative is increased in a value equal to half your Mind.

    Attack!: The target next Attack receives a bonus equal to your Mind, limited by your Tier,+3.

    Hold the line!: The Target receives Damage Reduction equal to your half your Mind score against the next Attack that targets them.

    Move! Move! Move!: The Target receives one extra Move action that must be used on their next Turn.

    Focus!: The Target next Opposed Test to resist a harmful effect, such as a Status effect receives Advantage. Focus can be used as a Reaction.

    Brace for Impact!: The target gains a bonus equal to half your Mind score on their next Block.

    Turn¶

    A Turn is the moment when a Hero must act. During a turn, the character has 3 Actions. When all actions have been used or the participant declares the Turn ended, the Turn ends.

    Round¶

    When everyone involved in the conflict has resolved their turns, the Round ends. Then a new Round begins, following the same Initiative order.

    These three steps represent the basic flow of combat, which repeats until the confrontation is over.

    Basic Combat Resources¶

    It is necessary to understand some of the resources a character has during combat: how Resolve is used, how Damage works, and the dynamics of Attack and Defense are fundamental to the progress of the game.

    Resolve¶

    Using this resource activates powers and other abilities, and it is also the first line against Damage. In terms common to other games, think of Resolve simultaneously as your “life” and your “mana,” but even so, if it runs out, a character may still keep going. It is the stamina that must be used strategically. When Resolve runs out, a Hero must mark a Condition. (See Conditions later in this section.)

    Attack¶

    These are blows, shots, cuts and everything else. To make an Attack, roll 1d20 + Accuracy and compare it to the target’s Defense. If the result is higher, the Attack is successful. See more about Attack under Combat Actions.

    Defense¶

    Defense is a character’s protection and their ability to avoid being hit. Do not think of it only as how tough a Hero’s skin or suit is; imagine it as the protective aura formed around them. A street thug would have a harder time keeping themselves from shaking long enough to shoot Batman than they would if they were shooting at a rookie vigilante.

    Tier Defense Value
    E 12
    D 15
    C 18

    Defense Value: A Hero’s Defense is equal to the base value provided by their Tier.

    Using Defense¶

    In combat, Defense is used when you are the target of an Attack, serving as the basic value the attacker needs to reach in order to hit you. Some attributes may be added to the base Defense value, increasing it against incoming attacks: Agility, Resistance, Spirit, and Mind may be used as protection against many different types of effects.

    Active Defense¶

    A Hero worth the name should always be ready to react to anything that is thrown, fired, struck or cast in their direction; that is where Active Defense comes in. With it, it is possible to add one of your Attributes to your Base Defense.

    Active Defense¶

    Activation: Reaction

    When using Active Defense, you prepare to receive an Attack. Choose one of the effects below and gain its effects until the end of the next Attack Action that targets you. A character may only have one Active Defense at a time. This Defense may be deactivated as a free action.

    Dodge: A Dodge is made to get out of the way of an Attack, adding Agility to the Defense value.

    Block: A Block uses Resistance to withstand the impact of blows that cause physical damage, adding Resistance to Defense.

    Damage¶

    Damage represents the harm and destruction a character causes (and suffers) during combat. Whenever an Attack hits, it deals Damage directly to the target’s Resolve. As a rule, a character’s basic Damage is equal to their Tier Damage Die + the indicated Attribute.

    Types of Damage¶

    The game separates different types of Damage into four categories, which interact in different ways with Powers, abilities and other effects during combat: Physical, Energy, Magical, and Psychic.

    These categories act as umbrellas, encompassing many different kinds of attacks under one label. Even so, one ability or another may not fit entirely into any of them; in that case, the Editor has the final word.

    Physical: The most common type of Damage, caused by blows, melee weapons and projectiles. This type of Damage is normally associated with Potency.

    Energy: Energy Damage includes pure energy sources or forces of nature, such as elements, energy blasts, light and shadows. This type of Damage is normally associated with Potency or Spirit.

    Magical: This type of Damage comes from magical sources, such as spells or artifacts. Magical damage uses Spirit as its Attribute.

    Psychic: Psychic Damage comes from sources that use mental power, such as telekinesis, telepathy, and illusion manipulation. The Attribute associated with this type of Damage is Mind.

    CONDITIONS¶

    Conditions are hazardous states that can be applied to Heroes over the course of a game. Conditions begin at “Scratched” and end at “Defeated.” Each time a Hero marks a Condition, they get closer and closer to defeat.

    Marking Conditions¶

    Generally, Conditions are marked when receiving a significant amount of Damage: perhaps that energy blast broke your spirit, or that cross punch knocked the breath out of you. There are some ways to mark Conditions:

    Choosing to Mark Them: When a character receives at least 10 points of Damage, they may mark a Condition, keeping their Resolve intact but absorbing the Damage directly. Even so, all excess damage beyond the 10 points negated in favour of marking a condition is still deducted from the Hero’s Resolve.

    Example: If a Hero receives 13 points of Damage from a Villain, they may mark one Condition to take only 3 points to their Resolve. In another situation, that same Hero receives 20 points of Damage, but this time they choose to mark two Conditions, absorbing 10 points of Damage with each one. Since there is no excess Damage, their Resolve remains intact.

    Condition Track¶

    Conditions follow an order and are not marked at random. In ascending order, they are: Tired, Scratched, Injured, Wounded, and Defeated. A character cannot mark the “Scratched” Condition without first having marked “Tired,” and so on. Some Powers grant extra Conditions, which must be counted before Scratched, while preserving the order of the track.

    Tired: The fight is dragging on, you begin to pant.

    Scratched: It was just a little scratch. There’s still plenty of firewood left to burn. Apply –2 to all d20 rolls.

    Injured: That one hurt. Not enough to stop you, but it hurt. Apply –4 to all your d20 rolls.

    Wounded: It is time to start worrying. Characters who are Wounded suffer Disadvantage to all their d20 rolls.

    Defeated: The Hero is out of combat and at the Editor’s mercy. Their fate will be decided by the course of the story.

    Fighting Without Resolve¶

    When Resolve is depleted, a character becomes Vulnerable: if they suffer 1 point or more of Damage, they must mark one Condition. In addition, for each multiple of ten taken while Vulnerable, mark one additional Condition.

    When in doubt, follow the table:

    Amount of Damage Conditions Marked
    1–10 1
    11–20 2
    21–30 3
    31–40 4
    41+ Defeated

    Example: Nightwing is out of Resolve points, which means he becomes Vulnerable. The Metal Sentinel he and the Titans are fighting lands a powerful strike, dealing 5 points of damage. Nightwing marks one Condition box. That one hurt!
