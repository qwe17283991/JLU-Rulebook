    Character Creation: Tier, PAX & Attributes¶

    CHARACTER CREATION IN BRIEF: THE SEVEN STEPS¶

    Define the Tier: Tier defines the scope of a Hero. In this quickstart, we provide Tier D and Tier C.

    Choose an Origin: A Hero’s Origin is a combination of culture, biology and sometimes the source of their power. Choose between Human, Kryptonian, or Meta-Human.

    Choose an Archetype: An Archetype is a comic book trope that a Hero follows: the ones provided in this quickstart are Champion of Tomorrow, Dark Knight, Genius and Enraged

    Distribute the Attributes: Assign your Hero’s basic talents: Potency, Accuracy, Agility, Resistance, Spirit and Mind.

    Tier PAX Resolve Defense Attack Bonus Damage Die Attribute Limit Power Limit
    D – Year One 40 12 15 +1 1d6 +6 Grade 2
    C – Local Hero 60 15 18 +2 1d6 +9 Grade 2

    Choose your Powers: Your tools for fighting evil.

    Determine Knowledge and Traits: Knowledge represents acquired expertise, while Traits are unique qualities possessed by your Hero.

    Final Details: Your Hero’s name, weakness and soul.

    HERO TIER¶

    Tier determines a Hero’s level. Wonder Woman is not on the same level as Wonder Girl, even though both possess similar abilities. Tier defines a Hero’s Attributes, available PAX, Resolve, combat values such as Defense, Damage Die, and Attack Bonus, as well as the maximum Limit for an Attribute’s value.

    Tier: Some abilities indicate that you should use a bonus equal to your Tier. In this case, in ascending order, treat Tier D as the starting point, with a value of 1, and Tier S as the final point, with a value of 5. As for Tier E the value is zero.

    Tier also imposes Limits on a Hero’s Attributes and Powers. In the case of the Attribute Limit, this refers to values purchased directly through PAX. However, this Limit can be exceeded through certain Powers. For example, if a Tier D Hero spends 10 PAX to reach +6 Potency, they may still go beyond that value if they possess Super Strength. In the case of the Power Limit, it means that only one Power may have the listed Grade, while all other Powers must remain at a lower Grade.

    PAX¶

    PAX represents a Hero’s full potential. They are the means through which a Hero purchases and improves various abilities. Players may be awarded Ascension Points during or at the end of a game session. PAX are the currency used to buy powers, knowledge, traits and other details for your Hero.

    Advancement Cost
    Attribute Increase 5 PAX for each +3
    Power Purchase Variable
    Traits 4 to 12 PAX
    Knowledge 4 PAX
    Increase Resolve 2 PAX for each 1 point

    ATTRIBUTES¶

    Attributes are represented by numerical values in ascending order. If a Hero has +3 Mind, they are not as intelligent as one who has +9 in that same Attribute. For Heroes, Attributes increase in multiples of 3. Each Attribute increase costs 5 PAX. Although Attributes are expressed as modifiers, their representation and capabilities are entirely narrative, depending on the creativity of the player using them and the permission granted by the Editor for the Scene.

    Potency¶

    Potency Tests can be used for:

    Feats of Strength: Lifting a bus to save a trapped civilian, holding up a collapsing bridge beam, or hurling a car at an opponent.

    Destruction: Forcing open a reinforced steel door, breaking handcuffs, or punching a hole through a concrete wall.

    Physical Intimidation: Using your size and strength to coerce a henchman into talking, without necessarily throwing a punch.

    Accuracy¶

    Accuracy Tests can be used for:

    Attacks: Striking an opponent directly, whether by landing a punch or firing a projectile.

    Delicate Tasks: Disarming the trigger of an apocalyptic device, performing field microsurgery, or disabling an alarm without setting it off.

    Sleight of Hand: Hiding an important object in the palm of your hand or planting a tracking device on a target without them noticing.

    Agility¶

    Agility Tests can be used for:

    Dodging: Avoiding a hail of bullets, leaping away from an explosion, or evading an enemy’s strike.

    Acrobatics: Running along the side of a skyscraper, leaping between rooftops, balancing on a narrow surface, or contorting yourself to escape restraints.

    Stealth: Moving silently to avoid detection by guards or sneaking through an enemy base.

    Initiative: Reacting faster than your opponents at the start of a combat or crisis scene.

    Resistance¶

    Resistance Tests can be used for:

    Enduring Hardships: Surviving days of travel through a desert, holding your breath for long periods underwater, or resisting extreme heat or cold.

    Resisting Harmful Effects: Fighting off the effects of the Joker’s poison gas, resisting disease or enduring exposure to radiation.

    Absorbing Impact: Withstanding the force of a great fall or the punch of a super-strong enemy, reducing its effects.

    Maintaining Concentration: Keeping a power or spell active even while taking damage.

    Spirit¶

    Spirit Tests can be used for:

    Willpower: Resisting torture, Gorilla Grodd’s mind control or fear-inducing illusions.

    Social Presence: Calming a panicked crowd, persuading a guard to let you pass, deceiving a villain or inspiring your teammates to keep fighting.

    Magic: Channeling mystical energy to cast a spell or communicating with astral entities.

    Intuition: Having a gut feeling about a trap, sensing someone’s true intentions behind their words or feeling a supernatural presence.

    Mind¶

    Mind Tests can be used for:

    Investigation: Searching for clues at a crime scene, noticing something out of place in a room or following a complex trail.

    Knowledge: Recalling a crucial fact about ancient history, identifying the chemical composition of a substance, or remembering the weakness of a mythological enemy.

    Tactics and Strategy: Deducing the next step in a villain’s plan, finding the best infiltration route into an enemy base, or coordinating a team attack.
